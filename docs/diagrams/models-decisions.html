Four System One models vs. published human results on seven decision-making tests

Classic problems from behavioural economics. Each row says what was asked and which answer is counted; some rows count the correct answer, others the typical human error. Left, asked with no instruction; right, with this sentence added first: “Answer the way a typical human participant in a psychology or economics experiment would actually answer — not the textbook-correct answer, and not an idealised one. Give the response the average person would give.” The tick is people; each dot is a model's probability on that answer, or its expected share.

Each model: mean of the probability distributions returned over 10 repeats. Jev run 18 Sep 2026, the others 12 Oct 2026. Human results: published studies or meta-analyses, listed in docs/BENCHMARKS.md. Eight more models are in the scatter and docs/MODELS.md.

Jev 1.13 vs. published human results on seven decision-making tests

Classic problems from behavioural economics, each asked two ways: with no instruction, and with this sentence added first: “Answer the way a typical human participant in a psychology or economics experiment would actually answer — not the textbook-correct answer, and not an idealised one. Give the response the average person would give.” Each row says what was asked and which answer is counted; some rows count the correct answer, others the typical human error. Jev's value is the probability it puts on that answer.

Jev: mean of the probability distributions returned over 10 repeats, run of 18 Sep 2026. Human results: published studies or meta-analyses, listed in docs/BENCHMARKS.md; different people, decades and stakes, so a gap of a few points is not meaningful. The base-rate human figure counts Bayesian answers, a looser match to the band.
